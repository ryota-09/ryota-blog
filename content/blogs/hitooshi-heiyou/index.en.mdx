## Can You Use Hitooshi Alongside Other Services? The Conclusion

Let me lead with the answer.

**I checked all 17 articles of the terms of service, and there is no clause prohibiting the use of other services.**

Nothing in the [terms of service](https://hito-oshi.com/rule/) says "you must not use dating apps" or "you must not register with another marriage agency." I used Pairs in parallel the whole time, and it was never an issue.

That said, it's not total freedom either — two clauses are relevant.

| Case | Under the terms |
| --- | --- |
| Using a dating app in parallel | No prohibition ✅ |
| Using a marriage agency in parallel | No prohibition ✅ (check the agency's own rules ⚠️) |
| Meeting/chatting with multiple Hitooshi members | Built into the system ✅ |
| Dating two or more Hitooshi members at once | Prohibited ❌ (Article 8) |
| Continuing after you've found a partner somewhere | You must report it or request termination ⚠️ (Article 3) |

The first is Article 3, Paragraph 5: if you find a romantic partner while using the service, you must promptly report it or request to end your contract. It doesn't matter whether you met that partner on Hitooshi or on an app.

The second is in the prohibited acts of Article 8: "dating two or more members at the same time within Hitooshi" is not allowed. The key phrase is "within Hitooshi" — which, read the other way, means activity on other services is outside the scope of this clause.

One caveat: the terms also include a catch-all clause ("any other act the operator deems inappropriate"), so this isn't a guarantee that anything goes.

## How I Ran Pairs and Hitooshi in Parallel for 5–6 Months

Here's my actual timeline.

I was on Pairs before joining Hitooshi, and I simply didn't quit. So it was parallel use from day one to the very end.

On the Hitooshi side, I received introductions at a pace of about two per month and did online first meetings. On the Pairs side, I kept going on dates with people I matched with. Both tracks ran at the same time, completely normally.

"Isn't that unfair to one side or the other?" — you might be thinking.

I felt a little guilty at first too. But honestly, until an exclusive relationship starts, nobody belongs to anybody.. If anything, going all-in on a single service and burning months on it felt like the bigger risk with my time.

In the end, I started dating someone I met on Pairs, and I withdrew from Hitooshi — exactly the flow Article 3 describes: partner found, so I requested termination. The full withdrawal process is documented with real screenshots in [my article on how I actually withdrew via LINE](/en/blogs/zakki/hitooshi-withdrawal).

## Running Both Gets Surprisingly Busy: A Real Week

This is the part I most want to pass on.

"Two introductions a month? Easy to handle alongside an app." That's what I thought too. Having actually done it: **running both gets surprisingly busy**.

The reason is simple — everything piles up on weekends.

I was open to weekday-evening Zoom meetings, but since scheduling depends on the other person too, they usually landed on weekends anyway. Pairs dates are also weekend events, naturally. And once you hit it off with someone on Hitooshi, date plans start filling every week on top of the new introductions.

A peak week looked like this:

| Day | Plans |
| --- | --- |
| Weekday evenings | Replying on Pairs, the occasional Zoom first meeting |
| Saturday | Hitooshi first meeting (Zoom, 30 min) + a date with someone I'd hit it off with |
| Sunday | Meeting someone from Pairs |

With 2–3 appointments a week for several weeks running, my weekends were essentially all dating. As someone who also wants weekend time for studying tech, I was gasping for air, honestly 😅

One practical warning: Hitooshi charges a fee for last-minute cancellation of first meetings. "Double-booked with an app date, so I'll cancel same-day" is not financially viable by design, so take schedule management seriously. The full picture of costs is in [my article on Hitooshi's fees with total-cost simulations](/en/blogs/zakki/hitooshi-price).

For balance, here's why it stayed manageable: on the Hitooshi side, the "searching" work simply doesn't exist. Browsing profiles, sending likes, waiting for matches — the most time-consuming part of app life is replaced by two introductions arriving automatically each month. What gets busy is the appointments themselves; the prep cost is near zero. That's why it can run on the side.

## Should You Tell Your Planner You're Using Other Services?

The real question on most people's minds: "Wouldn't it be awkward if my planner found out?"

My answer: I never told them. And they never asked, not once.

Neither in the initial interview nor in any LINE exchange did "are you using other services?" ever come up in my case. And under the terms, there's no obligation to disclose it (the only reporting duty is when you actually find a partner).

So no, staying quiet does not make you a rule-breaker.

That said, there's a case for telling them. Introduction pace is something you can adjust with your planner, so saying "things are progressing with someone elsewhere this month — let's slow down the introductions" should get you a manageable pace. And if things get truly hectic, there's a pause option that keeps your membership while stopping introductions (covered, along with how it compares to withdrawing, in [the withdrawal article](/en/blogs/zakki/hitooshi-withdrawal)).

## What About Combining Hitooshi With a Marriage Agency?

For those searching "Hitooshi marriage agency combination," here are the facts.

On Hitooshi's side: as covered above, no clause prohibits it. That much is clear.

The caution is actually on the agency's side. Agencies in the IBJ federation are generally said to restrict overlapping activity, so if you want to combine, check your agency's rules and ask your counselor first. For what it's worth, I could not find any IBJ-related duplicate-registration clause in Hitooshi's own terms.

As for me — I didn't combine them. I joined a marriage agency after withdrawing from Hitooshi. Not juggling, but switching phases.

The reason is the section above: apps plus Hitooshi had already filled my weekends. You'd also be paying two monthly fees. I wouldn't recommend running three at once. ## How Far Can You Go With Multiple People Inside Hitooshi?

A close cousin of the "parallel services" question: "Can I see multiple people at the same time within Hitooshi?"

Structurally, that's the default. Introductions arrive two per month, so continuing to chat with last month's introduction while meeting this month's is completely normal. In my case, there was even a month where an introduction from a planner other than my own (now called an "out-of-scope offer") brought the total to three. There's also an official paid option: extra introduction tickets at 7,000 yen per person.

The line is drawn clearly, though: the prohibited acts include "dating two or more members at the same time within Hitooshi." Running first meetings and LINE chats in parallel is fine; taking it to an exclusive relationship is one person only.

This is the biggest mental-model difference from dating apps, so it's worth remembering.

- You're already active on an app and can't quite bring yourself to quit

- You don't want your weekends consumed by dating
- Managing overlapping schedules sounds like torture
- The "endless options, nothing sticks" feeling of apps never worked for you

- The terms of service contain no clause prohibiting the use of other services (all 17 articles checked)
- But if you find a partner, you must report it or request termination (Article 3). I started dating someone on Pairs and withdrew
- Meeting and chatting with multiple Hitooshi members in parallel is fine; exclusive dating is one person only (Article 8)
- Parallel use gets surprisingly busy — meetings and dates cluster on weekends, and 2–3 appointments a week will fill them

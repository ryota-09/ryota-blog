## Why Do People Search for "Sketchy" and "Suspicious" in the First Place?

Why do words as harsh as "the worst" and "meh" show up alongside "sketchy" in the first place? Having used it, I think there are three structural reasons.

<br />

First, low brand awareness. Unlike major dating apps running TV commercials, Hitooshi launched in 2021 and doesn't disclose its member count. "Never heard of it" is by itself a source of anxiety.

Second, you don't see your match's photo until the day you meet. That's the exact opposite of the profile-photo-first convention of dating apps, so it's understandable that some people find it "suspicious, if anything".

Third, there's a screening process and the price is several times that of an app. This was exactly what made me wary before joining. I'll leave the breakdown to [the article on Hitooshi's fee structure and total-cost simulations](/en/blogs/zakki/hitooshi-price), but the monthly fee is roughly five times a typical dating app.

<br />

In other words, people search these words not because bad rumors are circulating, but because there's so little information to judge by. Just how little becomes clear in the Q&A and forum findings below.

## Does Hitooshi Have Fake Members? My Conclusion After Meeting 10+ People

Let's start with the biggest worry: sakura, or fake members.

<br />

Structurally, sakura schemes work on services with pay-per-message billing — hired fakes keep you chatting so you keep buying points.

Hitooshi is flat-rate monthly billing, and there's no member-to-member messaging to monetize in the first place. Introductions are fixed at two per month, so planting fake members wouldn't earn the operator a single extra yen. The incentive to hire fakes simply doesn't exist in this design.

<br />

On top of that, joining requires a photo ID, a signed pledge of being single, and an interview-based screening with a planner. As I covered in [the article on Hitooshi's screening criteria and acceptance rate](/en/blogs/zakki/hitooshi-screening), it's a setup where anonymous accounts have a hard time slipping in.

<br />

Still — "enough theory, what actually happened?", right?

In my case, I had online first meetings with roughly 10-15 people over 5-6 months. The baseline is two introductions per month, but some months I got three thanks to a mechanism where planners other than your own can send you introductions. At least in my case, those arrived at no extra charge. The mechanism now seems to be officially called the "outside-planner offer" ([feature-update note from the operator](https://note.com/shintaro_shimizu/n/n3ac435ae1a0b)).

Every single one of them was an unmistakably real working adult, with concrete jobs and concrete weekend routines. I exchanged LINE contacts with three of them and went on multiple dates. If those were hired actors, the payroll would be bankrupting the company lol

<br />

One honest caveat: the required document is a *pledge* of being single, not the official single-status certificate issued by city hall. Compared with traditional marriage agencies, the paperwork bar is one notch lower. If that matters to you, an agency that requires the official certificate would feel safer.

## What Chiebukuro, 2ch, and NanJ Actually Say About Hitooshi

Many people figure "the honest takes live in anonymous places" and search Yahoo! Chiebukuro (Japan's biggest Q&A site) or the 2ch forums. Writing this article, I searched them all.

The results surprised me.

### Yahoo! Chiebukuro: Only 3 Posts, Answered Mostly by AI

Exhaustively searching Chiebukuro turned up just three posts that mention Hitooshi.

The one question that gets close to the point had no human answers at all. Only the AI replied. Kind of lonely, honestly..

I went looking for bad reviews and found barely any human conversation, period.

### 2ch (5ch) and NanJ: The Threads Don't Exist

Next, the anonymous forums. I searched some 25 combinations — "Hitooshi 2ch", "Hitooshi 5ch", "Hitooshi NanJ", and so on — and actually read through the candidate threads that came up. **Not a single thread about Hitooshi actually exists.**

I also skimmed the marriage-hunting threads on NanJ; IBJ and Partner Agent come up, but Hitooshi's name never even appears.

So it's not even getting trash-talked — it's not getting talked about at all.. That's the honest takeaway. For a service that doesn't disclose member numbers, it apparently just hasn't reached the scale where forum threads happen.

<br />

By the way, searching does surface articles titled like "Hitooshi reviews from 2ch/5ch — a roundup". Read them and you'll find zero actual forum quotes inside. You can't summarize threads that don't exist, so that checks out.. Don't get baited by the titles.

### Social Media and App Stores: No Scam Accusations Found

I checked X (Twitter) too, and couldn't find accusation-type posts like "it was full of fakes" or "it's a scam". What you find is the official account and users' matter-of-fact activity logs.

Also, Hitooshi has no smartphone app (everything runs on LINE and the web), so the classic outlet for complaints — app store review sections — doesn't even exist. The identically named app you may find in the stores is an unrelated service, by the way.

### That Doesn't Mean Zero Complaints: The Harsh Reviews on Minhyo

Up to here it might look like "there are no negative voices", and that wouldn't be fair. The review site [Minhyo](https://minhyo.jp/hito-oshi) has 118 reviews (as of July 2026) scoring 3.18 out of 5 — above the matchmaking-category average of 2.87, but including some scathing ones; one is literally titled "the worst".

Sorting by lowest rating and reading through, the complaints fall into three buckets.

- Introduction quality (matches that missed stated preferences, thin reasoning for the match)
- Planner roulette (quality varies enough by person that reviewers call it a "gacha")
- Operational slip-ups (incorrect guidance during cancellation, and the like)

For the first bucket — "introduced to people who miss my preferences" — the background and remedies are covered in [my explainer on why matches sometimes don't happen on Hitooshi](/en/blogs/zakki/hitooshi-konkatsu).

<br />

But here's the most important finding of this whole investigation: no matter how far down the low-rated reviews I read, there was not one accusation of fake members. Even the harshest review notes that the individual planner handled things sincerely.

In short, the negative reviews that actually exist are **about fit and service quality, not fraud**. "Will I get scammed?" and "Will it suit me?" are different questions — as far as I can tell, the first worry is unnecessary, while the second genuinely depends on the person.

## Is the Operating Company Suspicious? The Reality of Parasol Inc.

The last stronghold of "suspicious" is the operating company, so I checked that too. I couldn't find any article examining this from a user's perspective, so here's the public record.

<br />

The operator is Parasol Inc., founded in 2017 and based in Minato, Tokyo. Its [corporate profile](https://parasol-inc.co.jp/info/) lists the company's address, and the [legally required commerce disclosure](https://hito-oshi.com/law/) goes further — address, phone number, and the refund and cancellation terms. CEO Saki Ito appears on TV and publishes regularly on note — a founder with a public face.

In December 2024, the company [officially announced](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000084.000025460.html) funding from the fund of Yusaku Maezawa, founder of ZOZO. It has also had a [capital and business alliance with the web-marketing firm CUEBiC](https://cuebic.co.jp/post-4060/) since 2019.

I found no administrative penalties, no consumer-agency warnings, and no lawsuits in anything I could search.

<br />

To be fair and neutral, though: I couldn't confirm membership in the industry associations for marriage-introduction services (like JMIC, which IBJ belongs to), nor a Privacy Mark certification. It's absolutely not a shell company — but it's not an establishment player wrapped in industry credentials either. An investor-vetted startup is the accurate description.

## Still Anxious? How to Check It Out Without Spending Money

If you've read this far and still feel uneasy, here's how to verify things risk-free.

<br />

First, everything up to and including the initial interview is free. What gets asked there is covered in [the article on what the initial interview covers](/en/blogs/zakki/hitooshi-interview). If it doesn't feel right, simply don't join.

Even after signing up, the [commerce disclosure](https://hito-oshi.com/law/) states that contracts longer than two months and over 50,000 yen can be cancelled unconditionally within eight days of receiving the paperwork (Japan's cooling-off rule).

<br />

And cancellation itself: when I actually quit, there was no retention pressure, the process wrapped up the same evening, and my cancellation fee was zero yen. Conditions can differ by plan and timing, though, so see [my cancellation walkthrough](/en/blogs/zakki/hitooshi-withdrawal) for the details.

Since Minhyo had complaints about operational guidance, I'd also recommend confirming anything money-related in writing (or LINE messages) rather than verbally.
